INTRODUCTION
================================================================================

The Odoo Manufacturing module (MRP) enables companies to manage production
processes from raw materials to finished goods. This manual covers complete
manufacturing workflows including product creation, bill of materials setup,
work center configuration, production execution, quality control, and
integration with Inventory, Purchase, and Sales modules.

================================================================================
PART 1: INITIAL SETUP AND CONFIGURATION
================================================================================

SECTION 1.1: ACTIVATING THE MANUFACTURING MODULE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Step 1: Login to Odoo 19
- Open your web browser
- Navigate to your Odoo instance URL
- Enter your username and password
- Click "Log in"

Explanation: You need administrative access to activate modules.

Step 2: Access Apps Menu
- Click on the "Apps" icon in the main menu (grid icon with 9 squares)
- Wait for the applications list to load

Explanation: The Apps menu shows all available Odoo modules.

Step 3: Search and Install Manufacturing
- In the search bar at top, type "Manufacturing"
- Locate "Manufacturing" module (icon shows a factory/gear)
- Click "Install" button
- Wait for installation to complete (may take 30-60 seconds)

Explanation: This installs the core MRP module. It will automatically install
dependent modules like Inventory if not already present.

Step 4: Verify Installation
- Main menu now shows "Manufacturing" icon
- Click "Manufacturing" to open the module

Result: Manufacturing module is now active and ready for configuration.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

SECTION 1.2: BASIC CONFIGURATION SETTINGS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Step 1: Access Manufacturing Settings
- Click "Manufacturing" in main menu
- Click "Configuration" in the top menu bar
- Click "Settings"

Explanation: Settings control which features are enabled in the module.

Step 2: Enable Work Orders
- Scroll to "Operations" section
- Check the box "Work Orders"
- This enables: Work Centers, Operations, Time Tracking

Explanation: Work Orders allow you to track production steps separately and
assign them to specific work centers (machines or workstations).

Step 3: Enable Quality Control
- Scroll to "Quality" section
- Check the box "Quality Control"
- This enables: Quality Checks, Quality Alerts, Quality Control Points

Explanation: Quality control adds checkpoints during production to ensure
standards are met.

Step 4: Enable Maintenance
- Check the box "Maintenance"
- This enables: Equipment management, Maintenance requests, Preventive maintenance

Explanation: Maintenance module tracks equipment health and schedules repairs.

Step 5: Enable Subcontracting
- Scroll to "Operations" section
- Check the box "Subcontracting"

Explanation: Subcontracting allows you to send materials to external vendors
who manufacture products for you.

Step 6: Enable By-Products
- Check the box "By-Products"

Explanation: By-products are secondary items produced during manufacturing
(e.g., sawdust when cutting wood).

Step 7: Enable Product Variants
- Scroll to "Products" section
- Check the box "Variants"

Explanation: Variants allow single product with multiple variations (size,
color, etc.).

Step 8: Save Configuration
- Scroll to top or bottom of page
- Click "Save" button

Result: All essential manufacturing features are now enabled.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

SECTION 1.3: CONFIGURING INVENTORY SETTINGS FOR MANUFACTURING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Manufacturing tightly integrates with Inventory. Proper inventory setup is
essential for smooth manufacturing operations.

Step 1: Open Inventory Module
- Click "Inventory" in main menu
- Click "Configuration"
- Click "Settings"

Step 2: Enable Multi-Step Routes
- Scroll to "Warehouse" section
- Under "Manufacture", select "Manufacture (3 steps)"
  Options shown:
  * Manufacture (1 step): Manufacture goods directly
  * Manufacture (2 steps): Pick components then manufacture
  * Manufacture (3 steps): Pick components, manufacture, then store products

Explanation: 
- 1 Step: Simple, direct manufacturing
- 2 Steps: Pick materials from warehouse first, then manufacture
- 3 Steps: Pick materials, manufacture, then move to stock (best for large operations)

For training purposes, select "Manufacture (1 step)" initially.

Step 3: Enable Lots & Serial Numbers
- Scroll to "Traceability" section
- Check "Lots & Serial Numbers"

Explanation: This allows tracking products by batch (lot) or individual units
(serial numbers), crucial for quality tracing.

Step 4: Enable Expiration Dates
- Check "Expiration Dates"

Explanation: Useful for perishable raw materials or finished goods.

Step 5: Save Settings
- Click "Save"

Result: Inventory is configured to support manufacturing operations.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

SECTION 1.4: SETTING UP LOCATIONS FOR MANUFACTURING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Manufacturing uses specific inventory locations for materials and products.

Step 1: Access Locations
- In Inventory module, click "Configuration"
- Click "Locations"

Step 2: Verify Manufacturing Locations Exist
- Look for these locations:
  * WH/Stock/Pre-Production
  * WH/Stock/Production
  * WH/Stock/Post-Production

Explanation: 
- Pre-Production: Materials staged before manufacturing
- Production: Where manufacturing happens
- Post-Production: Finished goods before moving to stock

Step 3: Create Custom Location (Optional)
- If you need a specific location, click "Create"
- Fill in:
  * Location Name: "Assembly Area"
  * Parent Location: Select "WH/Stock"
  * Location Type: Select "Internal Location"
- Click "Save"

Result: Manufacturing locations are ready for use.

PART 2: PRODUCT CREATION AND BILLS OF MATERIALS
================================================================================

Real-Life Scenario: You manufacture wooden tables. Each table requires:
- 1 Table Top (wooden board)
- 4 Table Legs (wooden legs)
- 20 Screws
- 1 hour of assembly work

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

SECTION 2.1: CREATING RAW MATERIAL PRODUCTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Step 1: Access Products
- Click "Manufacturing" in main menu
- Click "Products"
- Click "Products" again from dropdown

Step 2: Create Table Top Product
- Click "Create" button
- Fill in Product Details:
  * Product Name: "Table Top - Wood"
  * Can be Sold: Uncheck (this is a component, not sold directly)
  * Can be Purchased: Check
  * Product Type: Select "Storable Product"
  * Invoicing Policy: Keep default
- Click "Save"

Explanation: Storable products track inventory quantities. Components should
be marked as purchasable so procurement knows to buy them.

Step 3: Set Purchase Information
- Click "Purchase" tab
- Fill in:
  * Vendor: Click "Add a line", select or create a vendor "Wood Supplier Co."
  * Price: 50.00
  * Delivery Lead Time: 7 days
- Click "Save"

Step 4: Create Table Legs Product
- Click "Create" button (or back to products list and create new)
- Fill in:
  * Product Name: "Table Leg - Wood"
  * Can be Sold: Uncheck
  * Can be Purchased: Check
  * Product Type: "Storable Product"
- Click "Save"
- In "Purchase" tab:
  * Vendor: "Wood Supplier Co."
  * Price: 10.00 per unit
  * Delivery Lead Time: 7 days

Step 5: Create Screws Product
- Click "Create" button
- Fill in:
  * Product Name: "Screws - Stainless Steel"
  * Can be Sold: Uncheck
  * Can be Purchased: Check
  * Product Type: "Storable Product"
  * Unit of Measure: "Units"
- Click "Save"
- In "Purchase" tab:
  * Vendor: "Hardware Supplies Inc."
  * Price: 0.05 per unit
  * Delivery Lead Time: 3 days

Explanation: We've created all raw materials needed for manufacturing.

Result: Raw materials are defined and ready to be used in Bill of Materials.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

SECTION 2.2: CREATING FINISHED PRODUCT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Step 1: Create Finished Product
- Click "Create" button
- Fill in:
  * Product Name: "Wooden Dining Table - Classic"
  * Can be Sold: Check
  * Can be Purchased: Uncheck
  * Product Type: "Storable Product"
- Click "Save"

Explanation: This is your finished good that customers will buy.

Step 2: Set Sales Information
- Click "Sales" tab
- Fill in:
  * Sales Price: 299.00
  * Customer Taxes: Select appropriate tax if configured
- Click "Save"

Step 3: Set Product Route
- Click "Inventory" tab
- In "Operations" section, check:
  * "Manufacture": Check this box
  * "Make To Order": Optionally check (produces only when sold)

Explanation: "Manufacture" route tells Odoo this product is manufactured,
not purchased or dropshipped. "Make To Order" triggers production only when
a sales order is confirmed.

Step 4: Save Product
- Click "Save"

Result: Finished product is created and configured for manufacturing.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

SECTION 2.3: CREATING BILL OF MATERIALS (BOM)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A Bill of Materials defines what components and operations are needed to
manufacture a finished product.

Step 1: Create BOM from Product
- While viewing "Wooden Dining Table - Classic" product
- Click "Bill of Materials" smart button (shows "0" initially)
- Click "Create" button

Alternative Path:
- Manufacturing → Products → Bills of Materials → Create

Step 2: Fill BOM Header Information
- Product: Auto-filled "Wooden Dining Table - Classic"
- BOM Type: Select "Manufacture this product"
  Options:
  * Manufacture this product: Normal production
  * Kit: Components are sold together but not assembled
- Quantity: 1.00 (this BOM produces 1 table)
- Reference: Leave blank or enter "BOM-TABLE-001"

Explanation: BOM Type "Manufacture" means these components will be consumed
to create the finished product. "Kit" just groups items for sale.

Step 3: Add Components to BOM
- In "Components" tab
- Click "Add a line"

Component 1:
- Product: Select "Table Top - Wood"
- Quantity: 1

Click "Add a line" again

Component 2:
- Product: Select "Table Leg - Wood"
- Quantity: 4

Click "Add a line" again

Component 3:
- Product: Select "Screws - Stainless Steel"
- Quantity: 20

Explanation: Each line specifies what materials are consumed and in what
quantity to produce 1 unit of the finished product.

Step 4: Save BOM
- Click "Save"

Result: Basic BOM is created. When you manufacture 1 table, system will
consume 1 table top, 4 legs, and 20 screws from inventory.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

SECTION 2.4: ADDING OPERATIONS TO BOM (WITH WORK CENTERS)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Operations define the production steps and where they occur.

Step 1: Create Work Center First
- Go to Manufacturing → Configuration → Work Centers
- Click "Create"
- Fill in:
  * Work Center Name: "Assembly Station 1"
  * Default Capacity: 1 (how many products can be worked on simultaneously)
  * Time Efficiency: 100%
  * Costs per hour: 25.00 (labor + machine cost)
- Click "Save"

Explanation: Work Center represents a physical location or machine where
work happens. Costs help calculate production costs.

Step 2: Open Your BOM Again
- Manufacturing → Products → Bills of Materials
- Click on "Wooden Dining Table - Classic" BOM

Step 3: Add Operations
- Click "Operations" tab
- Click "Add a line"

Operation 1:
- Operation: Type "Cut and Prepare Wood"
- Work Center: Select "Assembly Station 1"
- Default Duration (minutes): 15
- Description: "Cut table top to size and sand edges"

Click "Add a line" again

Operation 2:
- Operation: "Assemble Legs"
- Work Center: "Assembly Station 1"
- Default Duration: 20 minutes
- Description: "Attach 4 legs to table top using screws"

Click "Add a line" again

Operation 3:
- Operation: "Quality Check and Finish"
- Work Center: "Assembly Station 1"
- Default Duration: 10 minutes
- Description: "Inspect assembly and apply finish coating"

Explanation: Operations define the sequence of work. Each operation has a
duration that impacts scheduling and costing.

Step 4: Save BOM
- Click "Save"

Result: BOM now includes both components and operations. Total manufacturing
time is 45 minutes per table.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

SECTION 2.5: ADVANCED BOM FEATURES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

SUB-SECTION: By-Products
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

If your manufacturing process creates secondary products (like sawdust when
cutting wood), you can define by-products.

Step 1: Edit BOM
- Open your "Wooden Dining Table" BOM
- Click "By-products" tab
- Click "Add a line"

Step 2: Add By-Product
- Product: Create/Select "Wood Sawdust"
- Quantity: 0.5 kg
- Operation: Select "Cut and Prepare Wood"

Explanation: For every table manufactured, 0.5 kg of sawdust is also produced
and added to inventory during the cutting operation.

Step 3: Save BOM

Result: By-products are tracked and can be reused or sold.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

SUB-SECTION: Component Consumption
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Step 1: Configure Component Consumption
- In BOM, Components tab
- Click on a component line to edit
- You'll see consumption options:
  * Consume: When to consume this component
    - Strict: Consume exact quantity (default)
    - Warning: Allow over-consumption with warning
    - Flexible: Allow any quantity

Explanation: 
- Strict: System won't allow manufacturing if exact components aren't available
- Flexible: Useful when component quantities vary slightly

Step 2: Set Consumption Timing
- Manual: User confirms consumption
- At operation: Consumed when specific work order operation completes

Result: Component consumption is configured for accurate inventory tracking.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

- Open a Work Center (e.g., "Assembly Station 1")
- Click "Edit"
- Note these fields:
  * Time Before Production: Setup time (e.g., 10 minutes)
  * Time After Production: Cleanup time (e.g., 5 minutes)

Explanation: Setup and cleanup times are added to each production run. If
manufacturing 10 tables, setup happens once, not for each unit.

Result: Accurate time calculation for scheduling and costing.

PART 4: CREATING AND PROCESSING MANUFACTURING ORDERS
================================================================================

Real-Life Scenario: You receive a sales order for 5 wooden tables. You need
to manufacture them.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

SECTION 4.1: MANUAL MANUFACTURING ORDER CREATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Step 1: Access Manufacturing Orders
- Click "Manufacturing" in main menu
- Click "Operations"
- Click "Manufacturing Orders"

Step 2: Create New Manufacturing Order
- Click "Create" button

Step 3: Fill Manufacturing Order Details
- Product: Select "Wooden Dining Table - Classic"
- Quantity: 5.00
- Bill of Materials: Auto-selected based on product
- Scheduled Date: Select production deadline (e.g., 7 days from now)

Explanation: Scheduled date helps plan when to start production to meet
delivery dates.

Step 4: Review Components and Operations
- Click "Components" tab
- System automatically calculates:
  * Table Top - Wood: 5 units
  * Table Leg - Wood: 20 units
  * Screws: 100 units
- Click "Operations" tab (if Work Orders enabled)
- System shows operations from BOM:
  * Cut and Prepare Wood: 75 minutes (15 min × 5 tables)
  * Assemble Legs: 100 minutes (20 min × 5 tables)
  * Quality Check and Finish: 50 minutes (10 min × 5 tables)

Explanation: System multiplies component quantities and operation times by
the number of units being manufactured.

Step 5: Check Availability
- Click "Check Availability" button
- System checks if required components are in stock
- Status shows:
  * Available: Green indicator
  * Partially Available: Yellow
  * Not Available: Red

Step 6: Confirm Manufacturing Order
- If components available, click "Confirm" button
- Status changes to "Confirmed"
- Work Orders are created (one per operation)
- Components are reserved in inventory

Explanation: Confirmation locks in the component quantities and creates
detailed work orders for production floor execution.

Result: Manufacturing Order is confirmed and ready for production.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

SECTION 4.2: HANDLING COMPONENT UNAVAILABILITY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

What happens if components aren't in stock?

Step 1: Check Availability Shows Missing Components
- After clicking "Check Availability"
- Red indicator shows unavailable items
- Click on red text to see details

Step 2: Option A - Wait for Purchase
- If components have "Buy" route set
- System may have automatically created Purchase Orders
- Go to Purchase module → Orders → Purchase Orders
- Check for pending orders
- Validate and receive goods
- Return to Manufacturing Order and "Check Availability" again

Step 3: Option B - Manually Add Stock
- Go to Inventory → Operations → Inventory Adjustments
- Click "Create"
- Add products and quantities that are missing
- Click "Validate"
- Return to Manufacturing Order and "Check Availability" again

Step 4: Option C - Replenish Stock
- In Manufacturing Order, while viewing missing components
- Some products may show "Replenish" button
- Click "Replenish"
- Choose:
  * Buy: Creates Purchase Order
  * Manufacture: Creates another Manufacturing Order (for sub-assemblies)
- Complete the replenishment
- Return to original Manufacturing Order

Result: Components are available and Manufacturing Order can proceed.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

SECTION 4.3: PRODUCING WITHOUT WORK ORDERS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

If Work Orders feature is disabled, you produce directly from Manufacturing
Order.

Step 1: Open Confirmed Manufacturing Order
- Manufacturing → Operations → Manufacturing Orders
- Click on your order (Status: Confirmed)

Step 2: Start Production
- Click "Produce" button
- Enter quantity to produce: 5.00 (or partial quantity)
- Click "Validate"

Explanation: This single action consumes all components and produces finished
goods in one step.

Step 3: View Results
- Status changes to "Done"
- Components are removed from inventory
- Finished products are added to inventory
- Click "Inventory" smart button to see stock movements

Result: Manufacturing is complete without detailed work order tracking.

PART 5: WORK ORDER MANAGEMENT
================================================================================

This section applies when "Work Orders" feature is enabled.

Real-Life Scenario: Production floor workers need to execute specific
operations in sequence at different work centers.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

SECTION 5.1: ACCESSING WORK ORDERS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Step 1: From Manufacturing Order
- Open your confirmed Manufacturing Order for 5 tables
- Click "Work Orders" smart button (shows "3" if 3 operations)
- List of work orders appears

Alternative Access:
- Manufacturing → Operations → Work Orders
- Shows all work orders across all manufacturing orders

Step 2: Understanding Work Order Status
- Ready: Can start immediately
- Pending: Waiting for previous operation to complete
- In Progress: Currently being worked on
- Finished: Completed
- Cancelled: Stopped

Explanation: Operations typically must be done in sequence, unless configured
otherwise.

Result: Work orders are visible and ready for execution.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

SECTION 5.2: EXECUTING WORK ORDERS (TABLET/KIOSK VIEW)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Odoo provides a tablet-friendly interface for shop floor workers.

Step 1: Access Work Center Planning
- Manufacturing → Planning → Work Centers
- Click "Plan" button (or Work Center Planning)
- Shows Gantt chart of scheduled work

Step 2: Open Tablet View for Work Center
- Manufacturing → Configuration → Work Centers
- Click on "Assembly Station 1"
- Click "Work Orders" button in the form
- Click "Open Tablet View" button (or directly navigate)

Alternative:
- Manufacturing → Operations → Work Orders
- Apply filter: Work Center = Assembly Station 1
- Switch to Kanban view
- Click on a work order card

Step 3: Start Work Order (Tablet View)
- You see work order details:
  * Product being manufactured
  * Quantity
  * Expected duration
  * Instructions
- Click "Start" button
- Timer starts counting

Explanation: Timer tracks actual time spent vs. planned time. This data
feeds into efficiency reports.

Step 4: Register Components Consumption
- If components need manual confirmation
- In "Components" section
- Each component shows quantity to consume
- Click "Register" or confirm consumption

Explanation: Manual registration ensures accurate tracking if quantities
vary slightly from BOM.

Step 5: Mark Work Order as Done
- After completing the operation
- Click "Mark as Done" button
- System records:
  * Actual time taken
  * Components consumed
  * Quantity produced

Step 6: Move to Next Operation
- System automatically makes next operation "Ready"
- Worker at next work center can start their work order
- Repeat process for each operation

Result: All operations are completed sequentially, tracked in detail.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

SECTION 5.3: WORK ORDER DEPENDENCIES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Some operations can run in parallel, others must wait.

Step 1: Configure Dependencies in BOM
- Manufacturing → Products → Bills of Materials
- Open your "Wooden Dining Table" BOM
- Click "Operations" tab
- Click on an operation to edit
- Field: "Start After Operation"
  * Leave empty: Can start immediately
  * Select operation: Must wait for that operation to finish

Example:
- "Cut and Prepare Wood": No dependency (starts first)
- "Assemble Legs": Start After "Cut and Prepare Wood"
- "Quality Check": Start After "Assemble Legs"

Step 2: Parallel Operations Example
If you add "Paint Table" operation:
- "Paint Table": Start After "Assemble Legs"
- "Quality Check": Start After "Paint Table"

But if painting and quality can happen simultaneously:
- Both start after "Assemble Legs"
- No dependency between them

Explanation: Dependencies control workflow sequence. Parallel operations
improve throughput.

Result: Operations execute in correct order with optimal scheduling.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Result: Quality is enforced at critical production steps.

PART 6: SUBCONTRACTING
================================================================================

Real-Life Scenario: You don't have painting equipment, so you send tables to
an external vendor for painting.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

SECTION 6.1: SETTING UP SUBCONTRACTING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Step 1: Enable Subcontracting
- Manufacturing → Configuration → Settings
- Enable "Subcontracting"
- Click "Save"

Step 2: Create Subcontractor Vendor
- Contacts → Create
- Fill in:
  * Name: "Premium Painting Services"
  * Company Type: Company
  * Check "Is a Vendor"
  * Check "Is a Subcontractor" (appears after enabling feature)
- Click "Save"

Step 3: Create Subcontracted Product
You have two approaches:

Approach A: Subcontract Entire Product
- Create product "Painted Wooden Table"
- Set route "Buy" (purchased from subcontractor)

Approach B: Subcontract Operation
- Add painting operation to BOM
- Set it as subcontracted

We'll use Approach B (operation-level subcontracting):

Step 4: Create Painting Service Product
- Manufacturing → Products → Products → Create
- Product Name: "Painting Service - Tables"
- Can be Sold: Uncheck
- Can be Purchased: Check
- Product Type: "Service"
- Click "Save"
- In "Purchase" tab:
  * Vendor: "Premium Painting Services"
  * Price: 25.00 per unit

Step 5: Modify BOM to Include Subcontracted Operation
- Open "Wooden Dining Table" BOM
- Operations tab
- Add operation:
  * Operation: "External Painting"
  * Work Center: Create/Select "Subcontractor - Painting"
  * Subcontracted: Check this option
  * Subcontractor: Select "Premium Painting Services"
  * Duration: 1 minute (minimal, actual work done externally)

Step 6: Add Components for Subcontractor
- Still in BOM, Components tab
- Add component:
  * Product: "Paint - Red" (create if needed)
  * Quantity: 0.5 L
  * Operation: "External Painting"

Explanation: Components assigned to subcontracted operation are sent to
vendor. Vendor uses your paint to paint the table.

Step 7: Save BOM

Result: BOM includes subcontracted operation.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

SECTION 6.2: PROCESSING SUBCONTRACTED MANUFACTURING ORDER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Step 1: Create Manufacturing Order
- Create MO for "Wooden Dining Table - Classic"
- Quantity: 2
- Click "Confirm"

Step 2: Complete Internal Operations First
- Execute work orders up to subcontracted operation
- "Cut and Prepare Wood" - Mark Done
- "Assemble Legs" - Mark Done

Step 3: System Creates Purchase Order Automatically
- Go to Purchase → Orders → Purchase Orders
- Find order for "Premium Painting Services"
- Order contains:
  * Service: Painting Service - Tables (Qty: 2)
- This is a dropship/resupply order

Step 4: Send Components to Subcontractor
- A delivery order is created automatically
- Go to Inventory → Operations → Transfers
- Find transfer to subcontractor
- Validate it (components leave your warehouse)

Step 5: Subcontractor Completes Work
- In real life, vendor paints tables
- In Odoo, validate Purchase Order when work is done
- Go to Purchase Order → Click "Receive Products"
- Quantity: 2 (painted tables return)

Step 6: Complete Manufacturing Order
- Return to Manufacturing Order
- Subcontracted operation is marked complete
- Continue with remaining operations
- Click "Mark as Done" to finish MO

Result: Subcontracting process is complete, components tracked accurately.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

SECTION 6.3: RESUPPLY SUBCONTRACTOR
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

If subcontractor runs out of materials and needs more:

Step 1: Create Resupply Transfer
- Inventory → Operations → Transfers
- Create manual transfer
- From: Your warehouse
- To: Subcontractor location
- Products: Paint, Screws, etc.
- Validate

Explanation: Manual resupply sends additional materials without creating new
manufacturing order.

Result: Subcontractor has materials to continue production.

SECTION 8.1: COSTING METHODS OVERVIEW
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Odoo supports three inventory costing methods:

1. Standard Price (Default)
   - Fixed cost per unit
   - manually set on product
   - Simple, good for stable costs

2. FIFO (First In, First Out)
   - Cost based on order goods were received
   - Realistic for varying purchase prices

3. AVCO (Average Cost)
   - Weighted average of all purchases
   - Smooths out price variations

Explanation: Manufacturing cost includes raw material cost + operation cost
(work center time × hourly rate).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

SECTION 8.3: VIEWING MANUFACTURING COSTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Step 1: Check Product Cost Breakdown
- Open finished product "Wooden Dining Table - Classic"
- Click "Costs" button (or view in form)
- Shows:
  * Components Cost: Sum of all component costs
  * Operations Cost: Sum of operation times × work center rates

Example Calculation:
Components:
- Table Top: 1 × 50.00 = 50.00
- Table Legs: 4 × 10.00 = 40.00
- Screws: 20 × 0.05 = 1.00
Total Component Cost: 91.00

Operations:
- Cut and Prepare: 15 min × (25.00/60) = 6.25
- Assemble Legs: 20 min × (25.00/60) = 8.33
- Quality Check: 10 min × (25.00/60) = 4.17
Total Operation Cost: 18.75

Total Manufacturing Cost: 91.00 + 18.75 = 109.75
Sales Price: 299.00
Gross Margin: 299.00 - 109.75 = 189.25 (63%)

Step 2: View Cost on Manufacturing Order
- Open completed Manufacturing Order
- Click "Cost Analysis" button (if available) or view in form
- Shows actual costs:
  * Materials consumed (actual quantities)

  * Total actual cost

Result: Cost analysis helps price products correctly and identify margins.

SECTION 8.5: WORK CENTER EFFICIENCY REPORTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Step 1: View Work Center Load
- Manufacturing → Planning → Work Centers Planning
- Shows Gantt chart of scheduled work
- Each work center shows:
  * Current load (% capacity utilized)
  * Scheduled work orders
  * Bottlenecks (over-allocated time slots)

Step 2: Analyze Work Center Performance
- Manufacturing → Reporting → Work Center Performance (if available)
- Or view from individual Work Center
- Shows:
  * Planned Time vs Actual Time
  * Productive Time (working)
  * Performance Loss (slower than expected)
  * Availability Loss (downtime, maintenance)

Step 3: Calculate OEE (Overall Equipment Effectiveness)
OEE = Availability × Performance × Quality

Example:
- Availability: 90% (90% uptime, 10% downtime for maintenance)
- Performance: 85% (took longer than planned)
- Quality: 95% (95% passed quality checks)
- OEE = 0.90 × 0.85 × 0.95 = 0.727 = 72.7%

Odoo can calculate OEE automatically if data is tracked.

Result: Work center efficiency is measured and can be improved.

SCENARIO 5: KIT vs MANUFACTURING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Business Case: When to use Kit (disassembly) vs Manufacturing.

Use Kit When:
- Components sold together but not assembled
- Example: Computer parts bundle (CPU + RAM + Motherboard)
- No manufacturing operation required
- Customer receives individual components

Use Manufacturing When:
- Components are assembled into new product
- Example: Assembled computer (CPU + RAM + Motherboard → Computer)
- Manufacturing operations required
- Customer receives single assembled product

Configuration:
For Kit:
- Create product "Computer Parts Kit"
- Create BOM with type "Kit"
- Components: CPU, RAM, Motherboard
- No operations

For Manufacturing:
- Create product "Custom Built Computer"
- Create BOM with type "Manufacture"
- Components: CPU, RAM, Motherboard, Case
- Operations: Assembly, Testing

Workflow Difference:
Kit:
- Sales Order → At delivery, kit is disassembled
- Individual components are delivered
- No manufacturing order

Manufacturing:
- Sales Order → Manufacturing Order created
- Components assembled
- Single finished product delivered

Result: Choose correct method based on business need.

BEST PRACTICES SUMMARY
================================================================================

1. BOMS
   - Keep BOMs updated with accurate quantities
   - Version BOMs for major product changes
   - Use product variants instead of multiple similar BOMs

2. WORK CENTERS
   - Set realistic time estimates for operations
   - Configure alternative work centers for flexibility
   - Track maintenance to prevent unplanned downtime

3. QUALITY
   - Define critical quality checks
   - Use blocking checks for essential quality points
   - Review quality alerts regularly to identify trends

4. INVENTORY
   - Use lot/serial number tracking for traceability
   - Set appropriate reordering rules
   - Regular inventory counts to ensure accuracy

5. COSTING
   - Choose appropriate costing method for your business
   - Review product costs regularly
   - Update work center hourly rates annually

6. PLANNING
   - Use MPS for demand-driven production
   - Balance Make-To-Order vs Make-To-Stock based on product type
   - Review production analysis reports weekly

7. INTEGRATION
   - Configure automatic procurement (Buy + Manufacture)
   - Link sales lead times to manufacturing lead times
   - Enable automatic inventory valuation for accurate accounting

8. PERFORMANCE
   - Monitor work center efficiency
   - Calculate OEE to identify improvement areas
   - Reduce setup times through batching similar products

9. DOCUMENTATION
   - Use operation instructions for complex procedures

================================================================================
COMMON TROUBLESHOOTING
================================================================================

ISSUE: Cannot confirm Manufacturing Order - Components not available
SOLUTION:
- Check Availability shows what's missing
- Click Replenish on missing components
- Or manually create Purchase Orders
- Or adjust inventory if components are actually available

ISSUE: Work Orders not appearing after confirming MO
SOLUTION:
- Enable "Work Orders" in Manufacturing settings
- BOM must have operations defined
- Re-confirm MO or create new one

ISSUE: Subcontracted operation not creating Purchase Order
SOLUTION:
- Ensure "Subcontracting" is enabled in settings
- Vendor must be marked as "Is a Subcontractor"
- Operation in BOM must have "Subcontracted" checked

ISSUE: Product costs seem incorrect
SOLUTION:
- Recalculate product costs from BOM
- Check work center hourly rates
- Verify component costs are updated
- Review actual vs planned times in completed MOs

ISSUE: Quality checks not triggering
SOLUTION:
- Quality Control Point must be active
- Product and operation must match
- Manufacturing Order must be confirmed
- Check frequency is set (All, Percentage, Periodic)

ISSUE: Inventory valuation journal entries not created
SOLUTION:
- Enable "Automatic Inventory Valuation" in Inventory settings
- Check product categories have correct valuation accounts
- Ensure products are "Storable" type

ISSUE: Cannot see Manufacturing module
SOLUTION:
- Install Manufacturing module from Apps
- Clear browser cache
- Check user access rights: Settings → Users → Select user → Manufacturing role

ISSUE: Lot numbers not appearing
SOLUTION:
- Enable "Lots & Serial Numbers" in Inventory settings
- Product must have "Tracking" set to "By Lots" or "By Unique Serial Number"
- Configure in product → Inventory tab

GLOSSARY
================================================================================

Bill of Materials (BOM): Recipe defining components and operations needed to
manufacture a product.

Work Center: Physical location or machine where manufacturing operations occur.

Work Order: Individual operation task within a manufacturing order, assigned
to a work center.

Manufacturing Order (MO): Instruction to produce specific quantity of a
product. Also called Production Order.

Make-To-Order (MTO): Production strategy where manufacturing only occurs when
sales order is confirmed.

Make-To-Stock: Production strategy where products are manufactured for
inventory based on forecasts.

Subcontracting: Sending materials to external vendor who manufactures on your
behalf.

By-Product: Secondary product produced during manufacturing (e.g., sawdust).

Kit: Product sold as a bundle of components without assembly.

Operation: Single step in manufacturing process (e.g., cutting, assembly).

Quality Control Point: Checkpoint that triggers quality check during
production.

Unbuild Order: Reverse of manufacturing - disassemble product back to
components.

Scrap: Material discarded due to damage or defect.

Backorder: Remaining quantity when MO is partially completed.

Lot Number: Identifier for batch of products manufactured together.

Serial Number: Unique identifier for individual product unit.

Lead Time: Time required to manufacture or procure a product.

Master Production Schedule (MPS): Forward-looking production plan based on
demand forecasts.

OEE (Overall Equipment Effectiveness): Metric measuring work center efficiency
= Availability × Performance × Quality.

Reordering Rule: Automatic procurement trigger when stock falls below minimum.

Route: Path defining how product is procured (Buy, Manufacture, MTO, etc.).

Valuation: Method of calculating inventory value (Standard, FIFO, AVCO).

Traceability: Ability to track product from raw materials through to customer.
